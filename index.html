A zero-lot-line lot in southwest Edmonton

The driveway faces the winter sunrise

Buying a house from a plan means committing to an orientation before anything is built. Which rooms get morning light, whether the driveway clears itself, when the back yard becomes usable again - all of it is fixed the moment you choose a lot, and none of it appears on a feature sheet. This is what the survey drawings and the weather record say about one of them.

The lot, through the year

Where the sun lands

Every window faces front or back

This is a zero-lot-line skinny home, and both long elevations are blank - soffit vents only, no windows at all. That single fact is why orientation matters more here than it would on a wide lot. There is no side glazing to compensate with. The frontage bearing is not one input among many; it is the only thing that determines interior daylight.

The floor plans then decide who gets which half. Kitchen, dining, living room, primary bedroom and ensuite all face the rear, so they take the long summer evening. Garage, den, foyer and all three children's bedrooms face the front, so they take the winter morning.

Try it on your own lot

The engine takes a frontage bearing and a latitude and works everything out from there. Turn the lot and watch which surfaces gain and lose. The bearing slider is instant because rotating the lot does not change what the house blocks - only where the sun is relative to it.

What the model assumes

The preliminary plot plan is treated as final. Where it defers a value, a standard assumption is taken. Each one below carries what it costs if it is wrong, measured by rerunning the model rather than estimated.
